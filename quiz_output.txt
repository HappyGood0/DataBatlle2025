Question: What is the technical character of a computer program?

A) It is not considered to have technical character.
B) It is considered to have technical character only if it has a direct link to physical reality.
C) It is considered to have technical character if it has the potential to produce a "further" technical effect.
D) It is considered to have technical character if it is not a program for computers as such.

Explanation: The correct answer is C. According to the Enlarged Board of Appeal, a computer program product might possess the potential to produce a "further" technical effect. This means that a computer program can have technical character if it has the potential to achieve a technical effect when run. The decision in T 424/03 also noted that the particular program involved had the potential of achieving a further technical effect when run and thus also contributed to the technical character of the claimed subject matter. Therefore, the technical character of a computer program is not necessarily dependent on a direct link to physical reality, but rather on its potential to produce a technical effect.

Question: What is the purpose of Art. 54(1) EPC?

A) To define the prior art
B) To prevent the state of the art from being patented again
C) To compare the invention with the prior art
D) To determine the content of the relevant art

Explanation: The correct answer is B) To prevent the state of the art from being patented again. The purpose of Art. 54(1) EPC is to prevent the state of the art being patented again. An invention can be patented only if it is new. An invention is considered to be new if it does not form part of the state of the art. The first step in deciding whether an invention is new is to define the prior art, the relevant part of that art, and the content of that relevant art. The next is to compare the invention with the prior art thus defined, and see whether the invention differs from it. If it does, the invention is novel.
